While watching the talk by Elder Bernard, I coudn't help but agree with the words that he was saying. 

AI can be a good thing. It brings so many good things. It can help with our ability to progress, learn, and innovate. However it is a double edge sword. AI can, when used incorrectly, be a detriment to our spiritual progression. 

Us, as agent engineers are aware of what LLMs are: token predictors. They just do an educated guess of what word is comming next from a vast variety of training data. LLMs don't feel, don't see, don't have a sense of self. They don't even have a sense of understanding of what it means to exists. LLMs don't see us as individuals, but, just as Elder Bernard said, rather sees us as an object to be acted upon. 

However the "object that is acted upon", from the perspective of an LLM, is actually a child of God. A child with a soul so precious, but at the same time imperfect. We feel, we make mistakes, we get hurt, cry, and struggle. We are vulnerable beings, and at the end of the day, we crave a conection.

Unfortunatelly, some people find said conections with Ai, which is concerning for their well beings. At the end of the day, Ais are not physical entities with the ability of empathy. It can fake it, but is never there. Therefore is our responsability to create walls around our agents so that the possible damage can be minimize as much as possible. 

It is our jobs that our agents are an asset to society, that they are productive. As members of the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-Day-Saints, we know that everyone is a child of God. We are all of divine potential. This gives us not only the perspective, but also the duty that our creations (in this case agents) are to be used to encourage people to continue with their personal progress instead of being of other's detriment. We should not worry about profits, but rather worry about and focus on serving others. 

I found an article published by Columbia University in how experts are cautioning againt the use of AI for emotional support. 

A lot of people, unfortunatelly, don't have the resources to get the help that they need, so they go to the best next thing that they can find — ai. However, according to the article and I agree, a fundimental flaw is that AIs tend to want to please the user. They are going to try to agree with the user and that can create a toxic environment where people heavely depend on Ai for their emotional wellbeing. A toxic environment so mad that according to the article, has caused the death of multiple teenagers that have engaged in relationships with AI companions. 

Additionally we already know of agentic hallucination. This can be extremely dangerous for people that will blindly trust AI. The LLM might give wrong information that can cause harm to a person. 

At the end of the day, AI can't replace humans. an AI can't be a shoulder to cry on. It can't give a person in need of a hug company or comfort. It can't — as talked in the sermon in the mountain — cry with the broken hearted. AI can never give a true sense of community that is vital for a human's emotional well being. 

As seen in this report, I don't think a good use for an AI chatbot is to seek emotional support.
